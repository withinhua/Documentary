## A. The cola wars and the decline

1. **Coke's share fell from ~60% (just after WWII / 1948) to roughly 22–24% by 1983–84.** [SCRIPT, as "around sixty per cent" and "under a quarter"]
   Sources: https://www.dawn.com/news/1329368 ; https://findvalue23.substack.com/p/study-coca-cola-and-pepsi ; https://www.historytools.org/resources/the-new-coke-failure-the-reintroduction-of-coca-cola-classic ; WIKI.
   Confidence: **Medium–High** on the trend. **Disputed exact figure**: 23% vs 24% (1983) vs 21.8% (1984); the base definition of "market" varies. Script avoids a precise 1983 figure beyond "under a quarter" (card shows "<25%").
2. **The Pepsi Challenge began in 1975, first in Dallas, Texas**: blind taste tests in malls/shopping centres with two unmarked cups; most tasters preferred Pepsi. [SCRIPT]
   Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi_Challenge (snippet) ; https://the-soda.fandom.com/wiki/The_Pepsi_Challenge ; https://scientificstrategy.com/cs-115/ ; John Sculley WIKI snippet.
   Confidence: **High** for 1975, **Medium** for Dallas origin.
3. **By the early 1980s Pepsi outsold Coke in supermarkets** (free-choice channels); Coke relied on vending machines and fountain/fast-food deals. [SCRIPT]
   Sources: WIKI "Cola wars" snippet; https://www.history.com/articles/cola-wars-pepsi-new-coke-failure ; https://scientificstrategy.com/cs-115/ (says from 1977).
   Confidence: **Medium** (start year differs: 1977 vs 1983).

## B. People

4. **Roberto C. Goizueta**: born Havana 18 Nov 1931; Yale chemical engineering (1953); began at Coca-Cola in Havana as a chemist; left Cuba in 1961 after Castro nationalised Coke's Cuban operations, reportedly with ~$40 and 100 shares of Coca-Cola stock; led the company from 1980/81 until his death in Oct 1997. [SCRIPT]
   Sources: https://goizuetafoundation.org/life-legacy/roberto-c-goizueta/ ; https://www.britannica.com/biography/Roberto-Crispulo-Goizueta ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Goizueta (snippet).
   Confidence: **High** (biography); **Medium** on "$40 and 100 shares" (foundation's own telling; script says "the story goes"). **Date nuance**: president from Aug 1980, chairman & CEO from 1981 — script says "took the top job in 1981".
5. **Donald R. Keough**, president & COO of The Coca-Cola Company in 1985. [SCRIPT] Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Keough (snippet); SNOPES; TIME85. **High**.
6. **Brian Dyson**, president of Coca-Cola USA (from 1978), and **Sergio Zyman**, head of US marketing (fresh from the 1982 Diet Coke launch), ran Project Kansas day to day. [SCRIPT]
   Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Dyson (snippet); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Zyman (snippet); https://marketinghistory.org/p/sergio-zyman-part-2-new-coke-end-of-marketing ; https://www.baltimoresun.com/1994/04/18/coca-cola-boss-gets-chance-at-comeback/ . **High**.
7. **Roger Enrico**, president/CEO of Pepsi-Cola USA in 1985; later PepsiCo CEO; co-wrote *The Other Guy Blinked: How Pepsi Won the Cola Wars* (1986). [SCRIPT]
   Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Enrico (snippet); https://archive.org/details/otherguyblinkedh00enri_0 ; https://www.foodmanufacturing.com/home/news/21098637/expepsico-ceo-who-helped-lead-cola-wars-has-died . **High**.
8. **Gay Mullins**, Seattle retiree, founded **Old Cola Drinkers of America** (formed 28 May 1985 per WIKI); set up a hotline in Seattle, sold anti-New Coke kits/buttons/T-shirts; filed a class-action suit. [SCRIPT]
   Sources: UPI1 ; UPI2 ; MF ; WIKI.
   Confidence: **High** for role; **Medium** for date and money figures (he reportedly borrowed/spent $100,000–$120,000 — sources differ; **not in script**). Membership claim "100,000" appears in KO (as the group's own claim) and MF. [SCRIPT, as "claimed"]

## C. Project Kansas and the research

9. **Code name "Project Kansas"** came from a famous photo of Kansas newspaper editor William Allen White drinking a Coke, used in Coke advertising. [SCRIPT]
   Sources: WIKI; https://the-soda-encyclopedia.fandom.com/wiki/New_Coke ; https://www.greatideasforteachingmarketing.com/new-coke-story-the-full-case-study/ . **Medium–High**.
10. **~190,000–200,000 blind taste tests.** [SCRIPT as "around two hundred thousand"]
   Sources: BRIT ("no fewer than 200,000"); HIST; KO; https://www.businessleader.co.uk/insights/how-new-coke-fizzled-out . **High** (figure is approximate; some sources say 190,000).
11. **The new formula beat old Coke and Pepsi in blind tests.** [SCRIPT] Sources: BRIT; HIST; DI. **High.** The specific split (e.g. 55%/45%, or 61% when branded) — **Medium/Low**, only case-study sites; **not in script**.
12. **Reported research/development cost of about $4 million.** [SCRIPT, hedged "reportedly"]
   Sources: https://conversion-uplift.co.uk/post/new-coke-market-research-fail/ ; https://www.business2community.com/consumer-marketing/market-research-fail-new-coke-became-worst-flub-time-01256904/ ; also the repo blueprint's example line. Confidence: **Medium–Low** (secondary business sites only).
13. **The research's blind spot**: because the project was secret, tasters weren't asked how they'd feel if the original were taken away. [SCRIPT, as narration, not as a quote]
   Sources: SNOPES; TIME85 (Keough's later admission); DI. **Medium–High** (interpretive, but consistent across sources). (WIKI also mentions focus-group minorities who were angry; not verified further — not in script.)
14. **"First formula change in 99 years"** (Coca-Cola dates from 1886). [SCRIPT] Sources: KO; https://www.powerhomebiz.com/marketing-tips/branding/lessons-from-new-coke.htm . **High**. (Nuance: the formula had minor tweaks over the decades, e.g. cocaine removal early 1900s; "99-year-old formula" is the company's own phrasing.)

21. **Consumer-affairs team grew from about a dozen to 180.** [SCRIPT] Source: KO (and http://www.coca-colacompany.com/stories/coke-lore-new-coke). **Medium–High** (company source).

## G. Outcome and the theory

36. **By the end of 1985, Classic was outselling both New Coke and Pepsi.** [SCRIPT] Sources: WIKI; https://www.historytools.org/resources/the-new-coke-failure-the-reintroduction-of-coca-cola-classic ; BRIT (Classic regained lead). **Medium–High.**
37. **New Coke fell to about 3% of the market** (some sources). [SCRIPT, hedged "by some estimates"] Sources: WIKI snippet; https://mwmblog.com/2021/04/22/new-coke-who-this/ . **Medium–Low.**
38. **New Coke renamed Coke II (1990 per most; 1992 per some), discontinued in the US in 2002.** [SCRIPT as "renamed Coke II in the early nineties"] Sources: https://abc7news.com/post/new-coke-ii-coca-cola-classic-history-of/5266029/ ; BRIT. **Date disputed (1990 vs 1992).**
39. **"It was deliberate" is a conspiracy theory; no evidence; company denies; Snopes rates it false.** [SCRIPT, framed as theory] Sources: SNOPES; https://www.mashed.com/1154317/the-fascinating-conspiracy-behind-the-origins-of-new-coke/ . **High** that it's unproven.
40. **Keough, July 1985: "The passion for original Coca-Cola — and that is the word for it, passion — was something that caught us by surprise."** Sources: SNOPES; TIME85 (variant "just flat caught us by surprise"). **Medium** on exact wording — not in script.
